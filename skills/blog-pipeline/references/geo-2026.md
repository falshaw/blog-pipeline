## The five things that changed in 2026 (act on these)

1. **Citation ≠ ranking.** The organic-rank → AI-citation link has collapsed: only ~38% of AI-Overview citations now come from top-10 pages (down from ~75% mid-2025). Ranking #1 no longer guarantees being quoted in the answer. *(2026 GEO vendor analyses — direction well-attested, exact figure indicative.)*
2. **Off-page entity beats backlinks for AI visibility.** Branded web mentions correlate with AI brand visibility far more strongly (Spearman ~0.66–0.71 across ChatGPT / AI Mode / AI Overviews) than backlinks (~0.22) or Domain Rating (~0.27–0.33); YouTube mentions are the single strongest signal (~0.74). *(Ahrefs 75,000-brand study, Dec 12 2025 — primary.)*
3. **Authorship became documented Google guidance.** Google added an **Authors** section to Search Central (Feb 1 2026) alongside the Feb 2026 Discover core update; named, credentialed authors gained Discover visibility, anonymous content lost ground. A strong `Person` + `sameAs` chain (LinkedIn / ORCID / Wikidata) correlates with AI-citation share. *(Google Search Central; Search Engine Journal, 2026.)*
4. **AI weights freshness much harder than classic Google.** ~half of AI-cited content is under ~13 weeks old; AI-cited pages run ~26% fresher than organic results; citation potential has roughly a one-year half-life. **Cosmetic `dateModified` bumps backfire** — crawlers compare snapshots and discount fake freshness. *(Foglift / Quattr / Getpassionfruit, 2026 — vendor, multiple corroborating.)*
5. **Zero-click is the norm, but citation pays.** AI Overviews cut position-1 organic CTR ~58% (Ahrefs, Dec 2025); ~83% of AIO searches and ~93% of AI-Mode searches end click-less (Pew; Search Engine Land, 2026). But brands **cited** in an AIO see ~+35% CTR vs uncited (Seer Interactive). GEO is how you recover some of the lost click.
6. **Citations went sentence-level.** Google's May 6 2026 AIO/AI-Mode overhaul places inline citations next to the exact sentence they support, plus an "Expert Advice" block pulling named first-hand perspectives from forums/reviews. Winners are pages that get *quoted sentence-by-sentence*, not pages that rank — which raises the value of extractable claim-→-evidence sentences even further. *(TechCrunch, May 2026.)*
7. **Schema is NOT a citation lever — measured.** Ahrefs controlled study (May 2026; 1,885 pages + 4,000 controls): adding JSON-LD produced no citation uplift in AIO (−4.6%), AI Mode (+2.4%, noise) or ChatGPT (+2.2%, noise). AI retrieval reads **visible HTML at fetch time**, not hidden structured data. Keep the core schema (Article/Organization/Breadcrumb) as machine-readable *identity hygiene*; never sell it as GEO. *(ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/.)*

## On-page citation levers (highest first)

The foundational measurement is the Princeton/Georgia-Tech/IIT-Delhi/AI2 GEO study (Aggarwal et al., **arXiv 2311.09735**, KDD 2024 — run on a Bing-Chat-era engine, so treat the % as directional, not 2026-measured):

| Lever | Effect on citation likelihood | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Add **statistics** | ~+41% | Hard numbers with a named source, above the fold |
| **Cite named sources** | ~+30–40% (up to **+115%** for lower-ranked pages) | Link the authority you're paraphrasing |
| Add **quotations** | ~+28% | A named expert quote |
| Fluency + authoritative voice | meaningful | Plain, confident, no hedging |
| Keyword stuffing | **negative** | Hurts in generative engines |

Plus the structural levers (2026 retrieval research — Lumar, Search Engine Land content-chunking guides):
- **Front-load the answer** — ~44% of LLM citations are pulled from the first 30% of a document, ~31% middle, ~25% end.
- **Self-contained 120–180-word chunks** with **question-format headings** earn more citations; AI retrieves at the passage/chunk level, not the whole page.
- **Explicit entities** — name the subject in each chunk; don't rely on "it / this / the above" (anaphora breaks chunk-level retrieval).
- **Comparison tables** are extracted preferentially over prose for "X vs Y" / spec queries.

## AI crawler landscape 2026 (the table to encode in robots.txt)

The 2026 shift: **search and training crawlers are now separate user-agents — you can block training while staying citable.**

| Engine | Allow (keeps you citable) | Training-only (block to opt out) |
|---|---|---|
| **Google** | **Googlebot** — powers AI Overviews *and* AI Mode *and* classic search (one index; never block) | **Google-Extended** (Gemini/Vertex training opt-out only; does **not** affect AI Overviews) |
| **OpenAI** | **OAI-SearchBot**, **ChatGPT-User** | **GPTBot** |
| **Anthropic** | **Claude-SearchBot**, **Claude-User** | **ClaudeBot**, `anthropic-ai` |
| **Perplexity** | **PerplexityBot**, **Perplexity-User** | — |
| **Apple** | Applebot | Applebot-Extended |
| **Common Crawl** | — | **CCBot** |
| **Others (2026)** | **DuckAssistBot** (DuckDuckGo AI), **MistralAI-User** (Le Chat fetches) | **Meta-ExternalAgent**, **Amazonbot** — allow if you want brand presence in those models |
| **Bytespider** (ByteDance) | — | The one worth **blocking**: heavy, largely non-compliant, no citation upside for most content sites |

*(Sources: No Hacks "AI User-Agent Landscape 2026"; Momentic AI-search-crawler list; widely confirmed.)*

**IndexNow (status re-confirmed 2026):** Google still doesn't consume it; Bing/Yandex/Naver/Seznam do. Still worth the one HTTP call per deploy — Bing's index feeds ChatGPT Search, Copilot, and DuckDuckGo, i.e. the AI answer engines. Keys live at the site ROOT (not `/.well-known/`). **Critical:** there is no separate "allow AI Overview" bot — blocking Googlebot removes you from AIO/AI-Mode and classic search at once. If your site sits behind a CDN such as Cloudflare, check whether its default AI-bot blocking is on and whether it blocks search crawlers too.

| **BreadcrumbList** | Live rich result | **Yes** |
| **FAQPage** | **Rich result DEAD** — stopped showing 2026-05-07; Rich-Results-Test + Search-Console support removed mid-2026. Google still *parses* the markup for comprehension. | Keep where it fits (comprehension/AI), **never** frame as a SERP rich-result |

## llms.txt — confirmed inert (don't sell it as a lever)

Now settled by two independent lines of evidence (as of 2026-07): (1) Google Search Central docs state outright (June 2026) that llms.txt is not used and has zero effect on Search including AIO/AI Mode; (2) the Originality.ai + Ahrefs server-log study (July 2026, 137k domains) found **97% of llms.txt files received zero AI requests in May 2026** — of the requests that did come, ~22% were SEO audit tools and only ~1% AI retrieval bots. Perplexity's "we read it" claim conflicts with the logs — treat as unverified. Keep the files current because they're free and some readers and tools expect them; never audit-fail a site over llms.txt, and spend real effort on content + author entity instead. *(Search Central; PPC Land / Originality.ai, July 2026.)*

## Image SEO 2026

- **Never lazy-load the LCP hero.** Use `fetchpriority="high"` and eager loading on the above-the-fold hero; lazy-loading the LCP adds ~200–400 ms (DigitalApplied; Google Web Vitals guidance). `loading="lazy"` is for below-the-fold images only.
- **Format:** WebP is the baseline; **AVIF** (now >90% browser support, 25–50% smaller) is optional via `<picture>` with a WebP fallback.
- **Responsive** `srcset`+`sizes`, descriptive **file name = slug/keyword**, concise contextual **alt** (≥20c, not stuffed), per-post **OG card** 1200×630 for social + AI-snippet thumbnails.
